# VISION

This project is a simple and elegant public home for a professional musician.

The purpose of the site is not aggressive marketing, personal branding, or audience growth optimization.

Instead, the site should:

- present the musician clearly and honestly
- showcase selected music and performances
- provide information about upcoming gigs
- offer a simple contact pathway
- create a calm and professional online presence

The site should feel like a thoughtfully prepared artist page rather than a commercial product.

---

# Overall Tone

The experience should feel:

- warm
- grounded
- mature
- understated
- human
- music-centered
- uncluttered

The emotional tone should communicate:

- musical depth
- authenticity
- quiet confidence
- experience
- care for craft

The site should avoid trying to appear trendy, youthful, flashy, or optimized for engagement.

---

# Visual Character

Visual inspiration comes more from:

- acoustic music venues
- literary spaces
- folk concert posters
- arts organizations
- public radio aesthetics

than from:

- startup companies
- influencer websites
- modern social media branding
- entertainment industry marketing pages

The visual design should emphasize:

- strong photography
- spacious layouts
- readability
- restrained typography
- subtle visual hierarchy


The site should feel calm and breathable.

## Color & Atmosphere

The overall palette should likely lean darker and more subdued rather than bright or airy.

The visual atmosphere should feel more like:

- an intimate evening performance venue
- a listening room
- a literary gathering
- a warmly lit acoustic space

than:

- a startup landing page
- a modern lifestyle brand
- a high-energy entertainment site

Preferred palette directions may include:

- charcoal
- warm black
- muted brown-gray
- deep olive
- warm neutrals
- subdued earth tones

High contrast should be used carefully.

The design should avoid feeling:

- slick
- glossy
- neon
- aggressively modern
- "dark mode tech"

Photography should strongly influence the emotional palette and visual tone of the site.

---

# Content Philosophy

The site should be lightly curated.

Only a small number of:

- songs
- videos
- photographs
- performances

need to be shown.

The goal is not to archive everything.
The goal is to present a clear and inviting artistic snapshot.

The strongest material should appear first.

---

# User Experience

Visitors should be able to:

- immediately understand who the musician is
- quickly hear or watch performances
- find upcoming gigs
- contact the musician easily

The site should remain simple and intuitive.

The experience should feel contemporary and polished without becoming flashy or technology-forward.

Interaction should feel minimal and respectful.

There should be:

- no autoplay media
- no popups
- no distracting interface elements
- no aggressive or overly theatrical animation

Subtle and restrained motion may be appropriate when it contributes to atmosphere and readability.

Examples may include:

- gentle fade-in transitions
- soft image movement or parallax
- refined hover states
- smooth scrolling
- slow and understated section reveals

Motion should feel calm, spacious, and nearly invisible rather than attention-seeking.

The music and photography should carry most of the emotional weight.

---

# Technical Philosophy

The project should remain:

- lightweight
- maintainable
- inexpensive
- durable
- easy to host

A static website architecture is preferred.

The project may still use lightly structured content organization and modest frontend enhancement where helpful.

Examples may include:

- structured content files
- lightweight rendering helpers
- responsive interaction patterns
- subtle client-side enhancements

The goal is thoughtful organization rather than framework-heavy application architecture.

The project should avoid unnecessary technical complexity.

Simple solutions are preferred over abstract or highly engineered systems.

---

# Long-Term Outlook

The site should be able to evolve slowly over time.

The structure should support modest future growth without requiring a major architectural rewrite.

Possible future evolution may include:

- additional project categories
- expanded media organization
- richer event handling
- more nuanced presentation of different musical contexts

The site should remain unified and coherent even as additional musical facets are introduced.

Potential future additions may include:

- calendar synchronization
- additional media
- mailing list integration
- expanded performance archives

However, the initial version should remain intentionally modest in scope.

The primary goal is to create something tasteful, stable, and genuinely representative of the musician and his work.
